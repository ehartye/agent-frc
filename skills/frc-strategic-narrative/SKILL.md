# Turn a field trial into a match strategy

Use the project-owned [rules model](../../docs/project-rules-model.md) and saved [field lab](../../docs/kickoff-field-lab.md) trials. When resuming a design, recover the current decision and most recent applicable test using [design continuation](../../docs/strategy-first-continuation.md).

Ask a student to describe one whole cycle in order: where the robot starts, which pieces it can reach in the chosen period, how it acquires and retains them, where it travels, how it scores and what happens if the lane or mechanism is unavailable. Link each step to a rule locator or an explicitly labeled assumption.

Compare at least two strategies under the same inventory and clock. Record pieces available by time, target capacity, scoring value, loaded and empty travel, handling time and the rate required during the remaining window. If serial handling misses that rate, compare staging, parallel paths or a different objective. Save the limiting term that would change the choice.

Infer competing corridors from starts, piece supply and scoring zones. Use space-occupying red and blue robots to test a choke point. Translate exposure into a retained-function requirement for the mechanism. Keep mass, approach speed, deformation and fastening assumptions explicit in an [impact load study](../../docs/impact-load-envelope.md).

Write the [strategy brief](../../docs/game-to-robot-strategy.md) in the student's words. State the fallback, the measurable requirement, its source and the next experiment. Record who selected the strategy before treating it as a design commitment.

When a design repeatedly loses an observation or pays a large cycle-time penalty to keep it, ask **could the premise be wrong?** Write the physical outcome the strategy requires and the assumption tying it to the current solution. Compare improving the existing solution, observing a different quantity and changing the mechanism or match action. Replay the same difficult case and a plausible failure for each alternative. Record what would change the selection. [Worked camera and retention comparison](../../docs/camera-reference.md#could-the-premise-be-wrong).

Carry the focal role, scored criterion, fallback and required window rate forward from the [matched alliance study](../../docs/season-meta-lab.md).

Attach the [exported role selection](../../docs/season-meta-lab.md#hand-the-selected-opportunity-to-the-robot-designers) when a strategic narrative becomes a whole-robot design requirement. State whether the witnessed action is acquisition, a credited placement or an opportunity still awaiting evidence.

When a role relies on a partner, [compare the complete alliance service chain](../../docs/alliance-completion.md) before selecting the robot architecture. Track the same piece from finite supply through delivery and reset on the match clock. Change partner availability and inspect which design completes more cycles.
