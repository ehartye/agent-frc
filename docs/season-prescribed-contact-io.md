# Three seasonal prescribed-contact IO experiments

This opt-in pipeline actually generates original native STEP sleeves for each season, rereads their mass properties, compiles a WPILib controller, executes raw encoder-count and digital-input/PWM simulation, and integrates sleeve rotation plus one translating game piece on a shared ideal electrical supply. It is **not** a calibrated capture, grip, launch, scoring or whole-robot simulation. All five-domain engineering acceptance remains blocked.

## Distinct inputs and observations

The 2024 NOTE uses the reviewed flat annular projection, native 101.6 mm diameter / 400 mm span sleeves and the manual mass interval. The 2025 CORAL uses its axial hollow-tube projection and different native 50.8 mm / 152.4 mm sleeves. The 2026 FUEL uses the explicitly chosen 150 mm metric nominal sphere and native 76.2 mm / 200 mm sleeves. The runner consumes exact current candidate graph, case, config, generated STEP and extracted-property bytes, not an inertia label. Each uniform sleeve density is an unvalidated design assumption; shaft, coupler, motor-rotor and gearbox inertia remain absent. Piece mass is an explicitly assumed midpoint of each source range, not a measured specimen.

The sole simulated beam is an ideal vertical ray at Y=0 and the last configured X sensor datum. A flat NOTE produces two blocked intervals with a clear center hole; the axial CORAL tube's walls block the ray throughout its finite axial extent; FUEL blocks over its diameter. Endpoint tangency counts as blocked using direct coordinate intervals. These are ideal nominal-shape projections, not an optics or deformation model. The other proposed sensor planes are not implemented sensors here. Actual controller DigitalInput sees only this boolean. It expects two exit pulses for NOTE and one for CORAL/FUEL, then stops PWM on trailing clearance. This is beam-sequence completion only: score and physical success remain unsupported. Plausible forged or intermittent beam sequences are not diagnosed; only declared initial-blocked, missing/stuck and timeout cases are tested.

## Prescribed forces, not discovered collisions

`references/season-contact-experiment.json` declares the finite symmetric engagement window, initial preloaded position, 5 N normal load per sleeve, friction coefficient 0.5 and constrained orientation/height. None is inferred from nominal CAD tangency. FUEL at a rigid nominal diameter gap is tangent only at its centered maximum cross-section; a finite powered interval assumes unmodeled compliance/preload or support. NOTE force is not switched off at its central hole: full-span sleeves may overlap the side rims. No off-window pickup or capture is modeled. Outside the imposed window the piece continues losslessly; that is not a realistic coast/flight prediction. Gravity, piece rotation, deformation, air drag, jams and support reactions are unsupported.

Let q_i be signed shaft angular velocity, sigma=(-1,+1), r the sleeve radius and v piece speed. Signed slip is sigma_i*r*q_i-v. After the explicit motor-torque step, a two-variable box-constrained impulse minimization uses A_ii=r^2/J+1/m and A_ij=1/m, with |p_i|<=mu*N_i*dt. Piece speed changes by sum(p)/m; shaft speed changes by -sigma_i*r*p_i/J. Thus accelerating the piece loads the actual sleeve/motor/bus state, rather than creating free piece motion. The symmetric native sleeves are required by this bounded implementation. KKT, force bounds and mechanical-only work/dissipation are checked. Projection dissipation is numerical, not measured tire or game-piece heat. Contact removal adds no impulse.

The existing signed ideal-bridge shared supply serves both motors. The upper shaft/PWM/current are negative for positive feed, while duty times armature current is positive battery demand. Active regeneration rejects, never clamps. Zero duty means ideal short-circuit braking, not a safe stop. The pinned WPILib NEO profile is used intact; conflicting manufacturer test conditions remain unresolved, and empirical Kt/Ke mismatch prevents a global electrical-to-mechanical energy-conservation claim. No current-limit firmware, controller losses, battery chemistry, temperature or calibrated friction is implemented.

## Actual controller boundary and checks

The offline-only controller reads Encoder distances with its configured radians/count, computes 20 ms differences, and reads DigitalInput. The plant injects independently fixed 2048 counts/revolution, not controller constants. Its intentionally monotone spin-up demand rises while measured surface speed is below 2 m/s and holds until beam completion, disable, low supply or 3 s timeout. It is not a general velocity regulator and may overshoot that threshold. No plant truth is given to the controller. Ideal constrained translation and symmetric normal loads do not establish full mechanism equilibrium or robot weight distribution.

Run `python scripts/run-season-io.py --output NEW_DIRECTORY --java-home EXISTING_JDK17 --gradle-home EXISTING_OFFLINE_CACHE`. It requires all three source/native configurations and records exact input/source/runtime/test/output hashes. The ordinary Gradle test task excludes this opt-in task and cannot imply it ran. Seven native checks, three fail-closed Python contract checks (including optimized Python), and eight actual IO tests must execute with no failures/errors/skips. Tests cover signed circuit algebra, analytic impulse, ideal shape/hole endpoints, distinct transport and 1 ms / 0.5 ms refinement, zero-friction timeout, signed encoder corruption, missing/stuck beam and disable. Refinement compares all aligned samples of piece position/velocity, both sleeve speeds, voltage and current. Development limits (0.05 m, 0.05 m/s, 5 rad/s, 0.2 V and 10 A) are not calibrated engineering tolerances. The new pipeline is not registered as accepted evidence in the physics gate.
