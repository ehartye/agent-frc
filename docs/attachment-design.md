# Design the attachment before drawing holes

A mechanism drawing needs to tell the builder which member takes the load, how the parts locate, and how to get them apart. Make those decisions in the blockout while changing the footprint is cheap. Record the selected pattern, stock, hardware family and the test that could make you change them in the project's interface drawing or design decision.

## Start with the purchased mounting interface

Open the exact purchased configuration and its installation drawing. Keep prescribed hole count, pilot features, spacing, orientation, grip and hardware. Trace the loads and receiving-stock assumptions behind the pattern. If the host cannot provide them, design an adapter or select another product. Compare alternative hole patterns on the custom side of the interface.

## Start at the load

1. Draw the moving assembly and the receiving structure in the same coordinates. Mark the mounting plane, faces that can bear, axes of motion and the worst occupied volume. Locate the mass and CG with payload at both ends of travel. Include speed changes, motor or jam reaction and any relevant external contact. Combine loads that can actually coincide. Carry ranges and signs through the mount, including reversal.
2. Move each force to the mount using its lever arm. Sketch how the joint resists translation, twist, peel and opening. Compression needs a seated face, uplift needs a retained path into structural stock, and lateral loads need a located key, bearing or a qualified friction joint. If a foot opens under reversal, recalculate using the contacts that remain. Mark which fasteners locate and which clamp.
3. Lay out at least two feasible patterns on the *actual* host faces. Compare row spacing, overlap, force direction and local stiffness. Dimension each hole center to the real free edge, bend, weld, slot and neighboring hole. Subtract the largest permitted hole radius and position error to find the nearest remaining ligament. Check both members for bearing, tear-out and net section. Check a projecting bracket for bending and prying. A longer lever arm only helps if both ends land on supported stock.
4. Pick a removable or permanent joint, then an exact candidate fastener family, head and retention method. A through-bolt needs a bearing land and nut access. A hollow member may need a fitted crush sleeve or backing. A tapped joint needs enough full thread in the selected material and tip clearance. A blind rivet needs an actual grip range, hole tolerance, space to form its tail and room for its setting tool. Use the selected supplier drawing and material condition for capacity. Keep the effect of preload, unequal contact or a missing fastener open until tested.
5. Choose available stock, alloy or grade, thickness and manufacturing route together. If deflection governs, first shorten the unsupported span or turn a deep section toward the governing bending plane. Compare the actual drilled sections and joint rotation, then check strength using the grade and temper after the chosen process. Locate all holes from a repeatable datum. Show how to hold and drill both parts, deburr, inspect and assemble them with the available saw, drill guide, brake, mill or other shop equipment. Include the actual wrench and driver approach, wire and wheel clearances, and a removal sequence beginning with the assembled machine.

## Check the complete attachment across its tolerance zone

For each viable mount, place the bracket stock, receiving member, fastener heads and tails, washers and adjacent moving parts at the same installed datums. Sweep the tool through approach, seating and turning. Then sweep the parts that leave together through the registered removal order. Record the limiting *pair*, location and clearance for assembly, operation and service. Keep the purchased pattern fixed while placing any permitted adjustment on the custom side.

Use the zone on the drawing to find the worst permitted **combined** shift and tilt toward each limiting face. Independent X and Y tolerances admit a corner. A circular position zone constrains their vector sum. Include any datum or clearance-hole take-up the stack actually permits. Check nonlinear faces and curved paths over the intervening range, and keep an unbounded range open.

In this synthetic stock check a 20 × 20 × 3 mm square bracket lies next to a fixed 45° lip. Its nearest outside corner has 0.60 mm nominal normal clearance. The mounting center can shift independently by ±0.50 mm in X and Y. Either shift alone closes 0.354 mm. The permitted (+0.50, +0.50) corner closes 0.707 mm and drives the bracket **0.107 mm into the lip**. A centered Ø8 mm socket still clears the same lip by 10.035 mm along its axial approach. That candidate needs a relieved bracket or a different location. A drawing that genuinely limited the same center to a Ø1.00 mm circular zone would leave 0.10 mm bracket clearance at its worst permitted offset. Carry the applicable zone and installed neighbors into the [placement check](checked-placement.md) and [service-order study](mechanical-methods.md#prove-the-removal-order).

## Worked example: removable side-mounted drive

This synthetic design mounts a 4 kg mechanism on a vertical web. In its adverse operating pose the CG is 120 mm to the right of the pattern center. The project specifies an effective downward acceleration of 25 m/s² **including gravity**, giving 100 N downward and a 12,000 N·mm clockwise moment. The force acts in the web plane and the moment is normal to it. A load that pulls the bracket away from the web needs a separate tension and prying calculation.

Use rightward +X and upward +Y. A first sketch puts four equal bearing-type fasteners at the corners of a 40 × 40 mm pattern. A second spans two supporting uprights with an 80 × 40 mm pattern. For a rigid, symmetric, close-fitting group of equal stiffness, the direct load per station is (0, −25) N. The moment-induced load at centered coordinates (x, y) is `Mz × (−y, x) / Σ(x² + y²)`. These are the demands the bracket applies to the host.

| Pattern, center to center | Σ radius² | Load at top right | Largest resultant |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 40 × 40 mm | 3,200 mm² | (+75, −100) N | 125 N |
| 80 × 40 mm | 8,000 mm² | (+30, −85) N | 90.1 N |

Choose the wider pattern for the first prototype. For the two uprights, sketch a 20 × 40 mm rectangular tube in both orientations. With a uniform 2 mm wall and uncut sections, orienting the 40 mm depth in the governing bending plane raises the centroidal second moment from 14,379 to 44,459 mm⁴. Select that orientation provisionally if the tool envelope fits. Recalculate at the drilled row using actual stock limits and the selected alloy and temper. In this sketch a 110 × 70 × 3 mm plate spans two continuous backing lands. A 4.6 mm maximum clearance hole and 0.5 mm center error leave at least 12.2 mm of material to the outer plate edges. Check the host edges separately. If the wide row falls on a slot or a thin unsupported lip, relocate it, add a backed gusset or change the stock. Retain the compact layout for comparison. Reverse the applied force and moment together for the opposite direction. With this symmetric model the signs reverse and a hole bears on its opposite side.

Select an M4 × 0.7 socket-head through-bolt with a washer and replaceable locking nut as the serviceable prototype family. Take the exact bolt diameter, washer and head from a compatible purchase drawing before drilling. Sketch the head, plate, host, backing, washer and nut in order, then choose shank and thread length from that stack at both thickness limits. On a closed tube, check the inner sleeve and the outer bearing footprint as a connected load path. A washer or sleeve small enough to pass through the host hole supplies no pull-through restraint. For each candidate, show the socket reaching the nut and the drive lifting free with the adjacent mechanism still installed.

The table ranks the pattern under one load-sharing assumption. Real clearance holes may load sequentially, and a thin web can bend. During prebuild, carry the calculated demands and tolerance limits into the editable design. Record the unverified bearing distribution, material strength and service assumptions. Issue a later shop test plan with the drawing: drill a representative plate and backing with the team's tooling. Inspect actual hole positions and tool access. Apply the signed design force at the specified offset in both directions and measure slip and permanent set against the project's alignment tolerance. If it slips too far, changes which holes bear, opens a face or deforms the host, revise the locating feature, section or footprint. Without a sourced fastener/host allowable and a qualified service load, leave strength and fatigue open.

On the selected assembly, verify [contact and signed support reactions](coupled-support.md), [hidden backing](hidden-backing-fit.md), [actual section properties](section-bending.md) and [fastener stacks](fastener-stacks.md). Bind shop assumptions to [fabrication capabilities](fabrication-capabilities.md) and verify the [removal sequence](mechanical-methods.md#prove-the-removal-order) on the installed geometry.
