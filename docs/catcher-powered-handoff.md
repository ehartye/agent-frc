# Catcher to powered-guide continuity

Nine fresh integrated trajectories retain the original catcher world's position, signed velocity, time and generic 0.5 kg body at the simulated release event. The receiving roller plant spins from time zero to that actual event time and accepts the existing body kinetic energy without resetting wheel state, inventing a second feed impulse, changing mass or turning velocity upward. Transfer is horizontal toward negative original-world X. The receiver's local zero is only a coordinate origin, not a teleport.

The previous 0.2 Ns external reverse-release impulse is STILL present upstream. The result is not a fully motor-generated release. The receiver still assumes prescribed symmetric normal forces and a constrained translating body.

## Bounded results

- NOTE/FUEL use a short 100 mm horizontal receiving guide; CORAL uses 150 mm. Original 300 mm reverse paths are rejected because they intersect the upstream intake envelope.
- The generic body exits at about -3.124 m/s for NOTE/FUEL. CORAL exits about -1.165 to -1.176 m/s, depending on actual arrival/spin-up time. These are not the representative season-mass results from the previous standalone experiment.
- 100 and 50 microsecond steps agree within declared test thresholds. The inherited body energy equals the upstream kinetic energy, downstream energy residuals remain below 2e-5 J, and source/end positions agree with the coordinate map.
- Failed release, commanded closed gate, changed mass, inconsistent clock, reversed direction and an implicit tilted guide are denied. No-contact control preserves speed rather than inventing powered acceleration.

## Important remaining geometry counterexample

For every season the finite body held at catcher center X=0.4 m already reaches the nominal receiving-guide entrance near X=0.3496 m. Therefore a fixed closed nip cannot be assumed isolated before center handoff. This simulation delays contact activation until center transfer but does not model an opening/closing nip actuator, finite wheel geometry or its swept collision envelope. Continuity of a scalar state is not proof of a physically buildable handoff.

A receiving nip that is retracted/open while the catcher holds the body, with verified body-tail clearance and a controlled closure, is a candidate next architecture, not an accepted design. The original catcher gate/actuator, brake and load-path gaps also remain unresolved.

## Evidence scope

The new atomic receipt binds actual executed classes, loaded DCMotor implementation, source dependencies and the run token in XML and runtime output. The original standalone exit and powered studies are freshly replayed at this source revision, with historical receipts preserved in predecessor commits.

The same time axis is used, but the upstream and receiver electrical systems are not coupled into one battery/load solve. Only the receiving plant plus transferred-body energy ledger is checked, not future energy of all upstream moving parts. Neither installed target transforms nor physical robot sensing is supplied. A horizontal exit cannot be silently rotated into the prior elevated NOTE/FUEL launch experiments. CORAL installed trough placement remains unresolved. All scoring remains unknown.
