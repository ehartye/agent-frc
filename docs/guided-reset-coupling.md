# Reset coupling: the gravity gate cannot remain tautly attached

This is a necessary-condition kinematic screen, not an assembled or selected mechanism. It follows the pinned Actuonix P16 Rev B evidence and exact Southco candidate from the previous study. No new catalog capacity or power-loss guarantee is inferred.

A direct rigid connection, or a taut lifting cable, prevents the proposed free gravity descent unless the actuator moves or backdrives. The previous P16 speed rejection and conditional backdrive resistance therefore still apply. A rotary latch does not solve this coupling problem.

## Candidate sequence and layout constraints

A hypothetical 1:1 cable routes from the gate over a fixed upper pulley to the reset actuator. The pulley, cable attachment, cable travel envelope and guide spacing are not yet dimensioned hardware. The sequence must separate these operations:

1. Lower latch releases only after its load is removed; reset actuator takes up cable slack and lifts gate.
2. An independent raised catch supports the gate. Its engagement must be independently established, not inferred from actuator position.
3. Actuator prepays cable slack while the raised catch carries the load. The cable needs a managed slack pocket that cannot snag the piece, guides, striker or gate.
4. Raised catch releases. Sufficient slack permits descent without loading the actuator. Lower latch engagement is a separate event.

The raised catch is not yet selected. The Southco lower latch and small M6 striker remain only candidates; the catalog does not resolve guide mounting, orientation-specific retention capacity, striker approach clearance or power interruption. Two latch positions cannot be served by one fixed striker receiver without a new mechanism.

## Falsifiable kinematic findings

The model checks 36 combinations using sourced 200 mm P16 stroke and 46/18 mm/s no-load speeds. Each 70.8/134.3/170 mm gate lift is evaluated with hypothetical 0/5/35 mm slack reserve, comparing taut and prepaid cable states. Zero slack blocks every full drop. The FUEL 35 mm reserve needs 205 mm, exceeding the 200 mm stroke. A 5 mm reserve fits the scalar stroke screen, but does not establish packaging or tolerance acceptance.

For FUEL with 175 mm prepaid slack, a complete take-up/reset plus prepay cycle requires 350 mm actuator travel in this ideal routing: approximately 7.61 seconds at 46 mm/s or 19.44 seconds at 18 mm/s. These are optimistic no-load travel times, excluding latch delays, loading and settling. No claimed fast release/reset capability follows from the earlier gravity fall time. Source duty-cycle limits also remain applicable.

## Power-off dependencies

Before raised engagement, the lifting cable and actuator carry load; their conditional holding specifications are not a complete gate restraint. During prepay, an unconfirmed raised catch can let the gate fall unexpectedly. During descent, slack is not a brake. During reset, a partly lifted gate and open lower latch are not the previous captured state. Cable slack, latch state, guide obstruction and actuator power must remain distinct observations in any subsequent controller model.

The next geometry work requires an actual guide, raised catch, pulley/cable specification and mounting layout. Guide bearing reactions must include gate impact and offsets; rated axial actuator force cannot be substituted for guide moment capacity. Native fit, calibrated dynamics, electrical integration, game legality and physical acceptance remain blocked. The current study intentionally produces no vendor-CAD assembly claim.
