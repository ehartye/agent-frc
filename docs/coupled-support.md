# Check an observed coupled support

A robot can reach the required height while its support path overloads or leaves a rotational degree of freedom. For a hook and bumper, the horizontal reactions can oppose each other and create the couple that holds pitch. Moving one reaction sideways can introduce yaw even when every vertical load still adds up. First identify the free body, the instant, the mode of operation and the question that could change the design.

## Replay the teaching fixture

For measured parallel bearing seats, the [installed support screen](operating-intent.md#test-a-compression-only-seat) also checks whether the combined same-time forces would require tension, shear or a pressure center beyond the measured footprint. Carry the reaction directions and full moments into any comparison with an added hold-down.

```sh
node examples/design-methods/make-support-fixture.mjs artifacts/support-demo
npm run mechanism:review -- coupled artifacts/support-demo/study.json
```

Two spring seats carry the vertical load, and an upper guide and lower brace resist pitch. The prescribed spring overlaps generate the reaction forces independently of the balance check. At the nominal pose the pads carry 40 and 60 N and the guides oppose each other at 25 N. Increasing the brace stiffness at the same pose raises its force to 30 N, crossing its 28 N classroom limit. Moving the CG forward produces 4 N·m residual pitch. Moving the upper guide sideways produces 2.5 N·m residual yaw while all net force components remain zero. These are separate experiments on one pose. A new settled pose requires a new run.

## Register the free body and the executed sample

The generated `plan.json`, `study.json` and native run files form a complete example. The study pins the plan and each JSON observation file by relative path, SHA-256 and JSON pointer. Freeze the body, revision, right-handed frame, reporting origin in metres, contact and applied load roster, original cases, duty for each case, design factor and justified force, moment and geometric tolerances. Use `whole-body` for the free body under gravity, or `incremental` to analyze the increment about a separate base-loaded state. Incremental wheel reactions alone do not establish continued wheel contact under the total load.

For each snapshot, read one time from one producer. Preserve its version, original solver or instrument output, transform and per-contact physical action. The native identity names the body, revision, case, run, frame, units `m,N,Nm,s`, time, mode, duty, reaction basis and actual contact/load IDs. The `map` reads the quantities through JSON pointers relative to the native sample or the named contact/load row. A producer with different field names can register its field semantics in `identity.channels` or `row.channels`. The study cannot replace a measured reaction with a required balancing reaction. A pointer and digest establish which bytes were used. Retain the reproducible original producer and verify that its declared semantics and force sign are correct.

Each point contact provides an ID, `kind: "contact-on-body"`, active state, world-space point, world force **on the reviewed body**, compression normal pointing in the permitted force direction, signed normal gap (separation positive) and friction coefficient. Specify the expected contacts even if one is removed. Record its inactive state and zero reaction. For compliant contact, supply the registered allowed compression and its basis when penetration exceeds the declared geometric tolerance. A negative normal force, friction outside the stated cone, or load at an open contact invalidates the assumed support. Contact patches with independent spin, rolling or bonded moments need their own physical model. This point-contact contract grants no free moment.

Applied loads are separate `external-load-on-body` rows with prescribed or measured basis, application point, force and free couple. Count gravity once from mass, CG and gravity vector. Identify modeled, measured or conservatively bounded mass and its basis in the native identity. A modeled mass only qualifies that scenario. Internal action/reaction pairs on the same free body cancel and must stay outside its external load roster. Use `null` for unobserved components. Record modeled zeroes only for a degree of freedom the model actually represents. A two-dimensional simulation can report an excellent pitch residual while leaving lateral force, roll or yaw open.

## Read balance, capacity and sensitivity separately

### Which forces can act from outside?

Draw the boundary around the entire moving assembly. Wheel-to-chassis bolts transmit internal load. A tire touching the floor, an opposing object or a physical test clamp can exert external force. Put their contact IDs in the commissioned plan and preserve the active state and signed gap on the same clock. Run the free impact, physical clamp and opposed squeeze as separate cases. Try giving a separated support a reaction or removing the only real restraint. A physically impossible case can still give a plausible member stress from a structural solver.

For a friction-limited contact, commission `maxFrictionCoefficient` and its `frictionBasis` on the planned contact. The reviewer checks the measured tangential demand against that coefficient times the same contact's compressive normal force. Record any measured or assumed coefficient and its range in the project. Tire contact during lift, impact or tipping calls for the actual normal force at that instant.

### Cross-check planar inertia against motion

For a rigid body moving in world XY about Z, add `planarBoundary` to the independently frozen plan. The ordinary `mechanism:review -- coupled` command then checks these quantities for every commissioned case. Freeze the member IDs, including payload and removable parts. Pin two separate sources using the usual path, digest and pointer references.

| Plan field | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| `memberIds`, `massLedger` | Complete, unique IDs. The ledger contains `body`, `revision`, `frame`, `units: "kg,m,kg*m2"` and `members`. Each member records `id`, `massKg`, world `centerXYM: [x,y]` and `centroidYawKgM2` about its own center. Use zero centroidal inertia only for a justified point-mass model. |
| `motionByCase` | Each case ID maps to a pinned motion sample. Match `body`, `revision`, `frame`, `caseId`, `runId`, `mode` and `timeS` to the reaction sample. Specify `model: "rigid-world-xy"`, `units: "m/s2,rad/s2"`, `cgAccelerationXY: [ax,ay]`, `yawAcceleration`, `basis` and `provenance`. |
| `massToleranceKg`, `cgToleranceM` | Reconciliation tolerances for the reaction producer's reported mass and center. Register their engineering basis and the existing force and moment tolerances. |

Use independently observed kinematics from the measured or simulated motion for `basis: "kinematic-observation"`. Preserve the original motion and force producers. A fixed condition imposed on the same solver that supplies the mounting reactions cannot independently establish a physical restraint. Mark commanded motion `prescribed-kinematics` and acceleration inferred from the applied wrench `force-derived-prediction`. Their numerical consistency is reported and their qualification stays open.

The reviewer assembles mass, center and yaw inertia by the parallel-axis theorem. It compares the reported momentum rates with `mass × acceleration` and `yaw inertia × angular acceleration` at that center. For example, two 5 kg articles separated by 0.2 m with centroidal inertias of 0.1 and 0.2 kg·m² give 0.4 kg·m² about their common center. At 0.1 m/s² and 0.05 rad/s² the expected demand is 1 N and 0.02 N·m. A fixture that reports zero demand at this instant fails the cross-check. The original complete wrench, capacities and missing components remain visible. Use the full spatial momentum-rate method for changing configuration, roll or pitch. Member stress, fastener strength and the validity of tire and structural models require their own evidence.

The review sums external force and each moment `(point − origin) × force + couple`. Static residuals should approach zero at the declared pose. For a dynamic sample it subtracts `m·aCG` and the time rate of angular momentum about the CG, transferred to the same origin. Supply actual `inertialForceN` and `angularMomentumRateAtCgNm` on that clock. Express a rigid body’s angular term in the common frame, including the gyroscopic term where applicable. Mixing peak reactions recorded at different times cannot support one free-body result.

For unknown coordinates or force components a native contact can record `admissibleBounds` with a physical `basis`. Its `pointM` and `forceN` arrays hold inclusive [minimum, maximum] intervals at the unobserved axes. The static reviewer conservatively encloses each wrench component. Excluding zero beyond the justified tolerance rules out the frozen pose and forces under that enclosure. Including zero does not demonstrate that a single simultaneous set of contacts exists. A hypothesized redistribution must identify itself as a counterfactual and cannot qualify as observed support.

An optional independent `rating` reference for a planned contact registers an allowable resultant force envelope for that contact, revision, mode and duty in N. Identify its evidence as a working rating, verified analysis, load test or teaching assumption. The rating file must be separate from the reaction sample. A teaching assumption describes only the invented model. Use it only if the envelope applies to every represented load direction and simultaneous component for the installed interface. An unpowered or passive rating is required for loss of power. Missing, differently directed or incomplete ratings stay open. Check individual members, anchors, actuators and failure modes with the [mechanism hardening review](mechanism-hardening.md). A sampled contact margin alone provides no assembly-wide strength or endurance rating.

For sensitivity, sweep one typed native parameter while holding all other registered settings fixed. Record a native scalar response with its JSON pointer and unit under `identity.metrics`. An optional project `maximum` and `limitBasis` show which settings cross the screen. A `resolution` comparison requires at least three strictly finer positive step sizes for the same observable and reports whether their total spread is within the project’s specified `maxSpread`. Two matching steps are a sensitivity observation. For contact discretization, solver integrity and complete-window outcomes, use the existing [contact convergence workflow](contact-convergence.md). Repeat the physically relevant adverse state and preserve the open hardware checks.
